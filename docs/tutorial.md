# Typewriter tutorial: your first hour

This tutorial is for people who have never used Typewriter. You do not need any other skill. If you can type, you can do every step.

You will:

1. Make a book with a chapter and a scene.
2. Write your first words.
3. Add a character and a place, and link them in your text.
4. Use notes, comments and the Board.
5. Check your work.
6. Make a backup, and export your book.

It takes about 45 minutes. You can stop at any step and come back. The app saves as you type.

When you want to know more about a screen, open the [guide to every screen](using-the-app.md).

---

## Before you start

**Open the app.** On Windows, double-click `typewriter.exe`. On Linux, run `./typewriter`. If you need to build the app first, read the [README](../README.md).

Three things that make it easy to relax:

- **There is no Save button.** The app saves while you type. Your work stays on your computer. No account and no internet are needed.
- **You cannot easily lose things.** When you delete something, a bar appears at the bottom with an **Undo** button for 12 seconds. The app also makes a backup copy of everything each day.
- **Nothing you do here is final.** You can delete the practice book at the end.

---

## Step 1: Look around

The first screen is the **Dashboard**. It is empty now. Later it shows your words for today, your streak, and a **Continue writing** button.

On the left is the **menu**. It has groups:

| Group | What is in it |
| --- | --- |
| *(top)* | **Dashboard** |
| **Story** | **Notes** (a notepad for ideas), **Write** (your book), **Board** (index cards), **Story arcs** (plot threads) |
| **People** | **Characters**, **Relationships**, **Family tree**, **Factions** |
| **Worldbuilding** | **World** (lore notes and places), **Glossary**, **Items**, **Maps & Paths**, **Timeline** |
| **Review** | **Checks**, **Statistics**, **Export & Backup** |

Click a name to open a screen. Try a few. Then come back to the **Dashboard**.

Three more things to know:

- Press **Ctrl+K** anywhere to **search** your whole project.
- The button at the very top of the menu shows your **project**. A project is one book or one series. You can have many projects, and they never mix.
- The outlined button at the bottom of the menu switches between the **dark** and **light** theme. Pick the one you like.

> **Tip:** you do not have to use every screen. A writer who only uses **Write** and **Export & Backup** is using the app well.

---

## Step 2: Make your book

1. Click **Write** in the menu.
2. Press **+ Book**. A new book appears in the list at the top left.
3. Type the **Book title** in the box below. Leave **Order in series** as `1`. (If you write a series, book two gets `2`, and so on.)
4. Press **+ Chapter**. The new chapter appears in the list under the book.
5. Click the chapter. You see **Scene 1** under it. Click **Scene 1**.

Now you have a book, a chapter and a scene. In Typewriter:

- A **book** holds chapters.
- A **chapter** holds one or more **scenes**.
- A **scene** is where you write. Each scene has its own place, point of view, and who is in it.

A short chapter can have just one scene. You do not have to think about scenes until you want to.

Above the editor you see two small boxes. The first is the **chapter title**. Type one, for example `The letter`. The second is the **scene title**. It is only for you. It does not print in your book.

---

## Step 3: Write your first scene

Click in the big white area and type. Here is a short example you can copy, or write your own:

> Mara found the letter under the old clock. The paper was yellow and soft, like cloth. She did not open it yet.

Try these:

- Select a few words and press **Ctrl+B** to make them bold. Press **Ctrl+I** for italic.
- The toolbar above the text has more: underline, three heading sizes, lists, quotes, and a scene break line (`―`).
- Look at the bottom of the window. It shows **Scene**, **Today** and **Chapter** word counts. They stay in view while you write.
- Look for **Saved** near the toolbar. It means your words are safe.

### Make it comfortable

- Press **Look** above the editor. You can change the font, the text size, the line spacing and the page width. The page changes while you click.
- Press **Focus mode** (or **F11**). Every menu disappears, and you see only your text. Press **Esc** to come back. Many writers use this for a first draft.

---

## Step 4: Add a character

Characters live in the **Characters** screen.

1. Click **Characters** in the menu.
2. Press **+ New**.
3. Type the character's name at the top, for example `Mara`.
4. Fill in what you know: role, looks, traits, backstory, goals. You do not need all of it. A name is enough to start.
5. Pick a **colour**. The app uses that colour for this character on the Board and on graphs.

Want a picture? Press **Add portrait** and choose a file. The app cuts a square out of the middle and makes it small.

Add a second character the same way, for example `Old Tom`.

> **Tip:** stuck for a name? Press **Name generator…** on this screen. It makes names in styles such as Elvish or Norse. Click a name to copy it, or press **＋ Character** to make a character with that name.

---

## Step 5: Add a place

Places live in the **World** screen.

1. Click **World** in the menu.
2. At the top of the list you see two buttons: **Lore notes** and **Places**. Click **Places**.
3. Press **+ New**, and type a name, for example `The clock shop`.
4. Pick a type (city, village, ruin, and so on). Add a short description if you like.

**Lore notes** (the other button) are for facts about your world: history, magic, religion, politics. You can add one later.

---

## Step 6: Link them in your text

Go back to **Write** and open your scene. Now you tell Typewriter who is in the scene, and where it happens.

**Link a name in the text:**

1. Click in the text where you want a name, and type **@**.
2. A list opens with your characters, places and notes. Type a few letters (`Ma`) to narrow it.
3. Press **Enter** (or click) to pick `Mara`. The name goes into the text as a link. It prints as normal text.

**Hold Ctrl and click** the link to open that character. On the character's page, **Appears in** lists every scene that names them.

**Fill in the Scene panel.** It is on the right side of the Write screen. It has:

- **Status:** idea, draft, revised or final. Start with `draft`.
- **Point of view:** whose eyes we see the scene through.
- **Place:** pick `The clock shop`.
- **Who is in this scene:** tick `Mara`.

It looks like a lot of work, but it pays off. The Board, the Timeline, the Checks and the Statistics all use this information. And you can fill it in later.

> **Tip:** if you write a name in the text but forget to tick the character, a warning appears in the Scene panel: **Named in the text, not in the cast**. Press **Add to cast** and the warning goes away.

---

## Step 7: Use the quick actions menu

Select some words in your scene, for example `the old clock`. A small menu opens above them.

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **B I U** | Bold, italic, underline. |
| **💬 Comment** | Leave a note to yourself about these words. The note shows on the right. |
| **@ Link** | Link the words to a character, place, lore note, faction or item. |
| **＋ Make new** | Make a new character, place, item or faction from the words. |
| **📝 To a note** | Copy the words to a new page in **Notes**, or to a lore note. |
| **🔍 Find** | Find these words everywhere in your book. |

Press **Esc** to close the menu.

Try **💬 Comment** now. Type a note, such as `Who wrote this letter?`. A card appears in the **Comments** panel at the top of the right side. When you have an answer, press **Resolve**. The mark on your text goes away.

---

## Step 8: Keep ideas in Notes

Not every thought belongs in the book. The **Notes** screen is a notepad for ideas, "what if" questions and plans. Nothing in Notes is part of your book. It is not counted and it does not export.

1. Click **Notes** in the menu.
2. Press **+ New**, and give the page a title, such as `What is in the letter?`.
3. Write anything. It uses the same editor as the Write screen, so `@` links work too.

---

## Step 9: See your book as cards

Click **Board**. Each chapter is a column. Each scene is a card, with its title, summary, status, place and word count.

- Type a short **summary** on a card.
- **Drag the grip ⠿** on a card to move it up, down, or into another chapter. The order changes in your book too.
- Click a card's **title** to open that scene in the Write screen.

Add a few more scenes with **+ Scene** and watch the Board grow. It is a good way to see the shape of your story.

---

## Step 10: Set a writing goal

Click **Statistics**. Under **Daily writing goal**, type a number of words, for example `300`. Press **Enter**.

The Dashboard and the Write screen now show your words for today against the goal. The **streak** counts the days in a row that you reached it. A day that you miss only breaks the streak when the day ends.

Want to work in short bursts? Press **Pomodoro timer** in the menu. It counts rounds of work and breaks (25 and 5 minutes to start, and you can change them). When a round ends, the window edge glows, so you notice even without sound.

---

## Step 11: Check your work

Once you have a few scenes, open **Checks**. It has lists that find problems you might miss. Do not try to fix everything. Look at one list, and fix what matters to you.

| List | What it finds |
| --- | --- |
| **Plot holes** | A person in two places at the same moment of story time. |
| **Unfinished** | Things that look incomplete: a character with no description, a scene with no place. |
| **Names** | A name spelled two ways, such as `Aldrick` and `Aldric`. It can fix all of them for you. |
| **Repeats** | The same word used twice in a few words, and sentences that start the same way. |
| **Style** | How much of your text is dialogue, very long sentences, and weak words. |
| **Loose characters** | Characters who are in no scene, in only one, or gone for many chapters. |

These are hints, not rules. If a row is fine as it is, press **Skip** or **It is fine**.

---

## Step 12: Protect your work

The app makes a copy of your data each day and keeps the last 7. But those copies sit in the same folder as your book. If the computer breaks, they go too. So make a copy of your own:

1. Click **Export & Backup**.
2. Scroll to the **Backup** section. Press **Back up my data…**.
3. Pick a place, such as a USB drive, and save the file.

The app reminds you on the Dashboard when your last backup is old.

**If you make a mistake:**

- **In one scene:** press **History** above the editor. It lists old copies of the scene. Click one to read it, and press **Go back to this** to put it back.
- **For the whole book:** press **Snapshots** and save one with a name, such as `before the big rewrite`. Later you can see which scenes changed, and put an old scene back.
- **Right after a delete:** press **Undo** in the bar at the bottom.

---

## Step 13: Export your book

1. Click **Export & Backup**.
2. Choose **Manuscript**.
3. Pick the book. Type your **author name** if you want it on the title page.
4. If you like, open **Pages and style**. You can add a dedication, a copyright page, acknowledgments, and an "about the author" page. You can also choose the mark between scenes and the look of chapter headings.
5. Choose a file type:
   - **Word (.docx)** to edit in Word or to send to an editor.
   - **PDF (.pdf)** to read or print.
   - **E-book (.epub)** for e-readers and phones.
   - **Markdown (.md)** for other writing tools.
6. Press **Export…** and pick where to save the file. Open it to see your book.

Each chapter starts on a new page. A table of contents comes after the title page. You can turn it off with the **Table of contents** tick box.

## Where to go next

You now know the main loop: **write**, **link**, **check**, **back up**, **export**. When you are ready for more, the [guide to every screen](using-the-app.md) explains these:

- **Plan:** [Story arcs](using-the-app.md#story-arcs) follow your plot threads. [Revision passes](using-the-app.md#revision-passes) let you tick each scene off as you revise. [Scene tags](using-the-app.md#tags-on-scenes) group scenes, for example all the flashbacks.
- **Build your world:** [Relationships](using-the-app.md#relationships), a [Family tree](using-the-app.md#family-tree), [Factions](using-the-app.md#factions), [Items](using-the-app.md#items) that move through the story, and a [Glossary](using-the-app.md#glossary) for made-up words.
- **Maps and time:** [draw a map](using-the-app.md#drawing-a-map), draw each character's route, and use the [Timeline](using-the-app.md#timeline) with your own calendar. The app can warn you when a trip is too long for the story time you gave it.
- **Write better:** [Split and merge scenes](using-the-app.md#split-and-merge-scenes), and a [Mood board](using-the-app.md#mood-board) of pictures for each character, place or scene.
- **Several books:** a new [project](../README.md#projects) keeps a separate book or series.

### Questions beginners ask

**Where is my book stored?** In one file on your computer. The path shows at the bottom of the left menu. See [Your data](../README.md#your-data).

**Did it save?** Yes. The app saves as you type. **Saved** near the toolbar shows that it did.

**I pressed something and it changed. How do I go back?** Press **Ctrl+Z** in the text. After a delete, press **Undo** in the bar at the bottom. For an old version of a scene, use **History**.

**Do I need to fill in every field?** No. Fill in what helps you. Empty fields only show up in the **Unfinished** list of the Checks screen, and you can skip any row.

**Can I use it for something that is not a novel?** Yes. A short story, a screenplay outline, a role-playing game world, or notes for a series all fit.

**Does it need the internet?** No. It never sends your writing anywhere.

Happy writing!
